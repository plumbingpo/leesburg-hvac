From the hallway, a furnace that will not heat looks the same no matter what broke. Inside the cabinet, though, the sequence is strict: inducer on, pressure switch closed, igniter glowing, gas valve open, burners lit, flame proven, and finally the blower sending heat through the house. Most of the diagnosis comes down to finding the broken link. In Leesburg homes, it tends to be the same handful of culprits: a flame sensor coated in oxide, a hot surface igniter cracked by thermal cycling, a pressure switch held open by a tired inducer or a plugged condensate trap, or a control board with a burned relay.

Our experienced technicians diagnose a furnace the way it operates, in order, with a meter at every step. That means igniter resistance, microamp flame signal, pressure switch operation under actual inducer draft, gas valve voltage, and blower and capacitor condition. Safety checks are never optional on a gas appliance: heat exchanger inspection, leak testing on the gas connections, and flue draft verification, since a hazard is not visible at the thermostat. We finish by checking temperature rise against the data plate, making sure the burner and blower are matched the way the manufacturer designed them to.

We work on gas, propane, and electric models from every major brand, both conventional 80 percent units and condensing high-efficiency models with their traps, drains, and dual pressure switches. The truck stock covers the parts that actually fail: igniters, flame sensors, pressure switches, capacitors, inducer assemblies, and blower motors, so most no-heat calls end with heat the same visit. Signs You Need Furnace Repair in Leesburg

The sooner you notice these red flags, the easier and cheaper the problem is to resolve. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Repeated shutdowns just seconds after the burners light
  • A fan that keeps running while the registers blow cool air
  • Yellow, wavering burner flames where they should be sharp blue
  • Soot, rust, or moisture near the furnace cabinet or vent pipe
  • Grinding or high-pitched squeals from the blower
  • Short cycling every few minutes without reaching the setpoint
  • The igniter glows and clicks, yet no flame ever appears
  • Loud booms at startup caused by delayed ignition

The Benefits of Professional Furnace Repair

A furnace combines gas, flame, high voltage, and a flue in one cabinet, and the failure that matters most is the one you cannot see from outside: a compromised heat exchanger passing combustion gases into the air your blower pushes through the house. Delayed ignition that booms at light-off stresses that exchanger further every morning it goes unfixed. Meanwhile the diagnostic traps stack up: a furnace that trips its high limit may have a bad switch, or it may be protecting itself from a clogged filter or a dying blower, and replacing the switch in the second case just removes a safety while leaving the fault.

Our techs look at every symptom in the context of the whole furnace. We measure flame signal instead of assuming it, which keeps us from replacing a healthy sensor while a cracked igniter or weak gas valve slips past. Draft is confirmed at the pressure switch and at the flue. Temperature rise is checked against the data plate, catching airflow problems before they shorten the life of the heat exchanger. The gas side is leak-tested with instruments, never with a quick sniff. What you get is a furnace proven safe over a full cycle, plus an honest opinion if the unit is getting close to the point where replacing it beats repairing it again.

Maintenance Tips for Furnace Repair

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly heating and cooling problems down the road. Put these technician-approved steps to work around your property:

  • Notice how the furnace sounds at startup, because a quiet light-off is healthy and a boom is not
  • Store laundry supplies, especially chlorine products, well away from the furnace closet
  • On high-efficiency models, keep the condensate trap and drain line clear and draining
  • Book a fall tune-up so weak igniters and fouled sensors are caught before the first freeze
  • Replace the filter every month while the furnace is working hard, since it costs less than any other part
  • Give the furnace room to breathe, since a crowded utility space starves the burners of air
  • Keep a working carbon monoxide alarm on every level of the home and check it each month
